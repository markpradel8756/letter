Dear you,

I've started this letter more times than I can count. The words never felt quite right. Maybe some feelings are easier to carry than to explain. Still, I wanted to try, at least once, to put them on paper honestly.

I still remember the trip my family took to Mawab during the COVID era. We went with my mother's friend, Roselyn Malabo, to visit her mother's house in their hometown. It was supposed to be a simple trip: a little rest, a massage, a change of scenery.

Then we attended a meeting at your Kingdom Hall. And that was the first time I saw you.

Afterward, we took a group photo together, my family and the members of your congregation, all standing side by side. Among all the people there, I noticed you. I can't fully explain what happened in that moment, but something about you stayed with me. I never expected that one simple encounter would become a memory I would carry for years.

At first, it was just admiration, the quiet kind you keep to yourself. But as time went on, it slowly grew into something deeper.

Little by little, our conversations became a friendship I never saw coming. We talked about school and schoolwork, about our families, music, anime, photography, and all the little things that filled our days. We joked around, teased each other, exchanged compliments, and shared songs and pictures. Sometimes we talked about deeper things too: love, friendships, past experiences, personal struggles, and how important it is to choose someone who shares our spiritual values.

And there's something I have to say: you have a beautiful voice. I still remember the voice messages you sent me on Messenger of you singing. Even through a short recording, your voice stayed with me, and those moments became some of the little things I hold dear. And your drawings are beautiful too. You have a real gift, and I've always admired it.

There were times when you felt insecure, or when you were carrying problems that weighed on you. In those moments, I tried my best to remind you of your worth, and to remind you that you can always trust Jehovah. I meant every word. And thank you for opening up to me, for letting me listen, and for sharing your thoughts and your everyday moments. Through them, I came to understand more about who you are and the things that matter to you.

What I treasure most isn't only what we talked about. It's the comfort and trust that quietly grew between us. Even our simplest conversations, about schoolwork, random thoughts, something funny, or our faith, became memories worth keeping.

I know we haven't had many chances to see each other in person. Distance and the responsibilities we each carry make it hard to spend time together, and sometimes even hard to talk regularly. But I've never stopped appreciating our conversations and the memories we've made.

And here's something I've never really said: whenever I see your photos, especially the ones where you're facing away or your face isn't fully shown, I'm reminded of a faceless dream girl I used to imagine long before I ever met you. It's a strange feeling, but a beautiful one, that someone I once only pictured in my dreams could remind me of someone real.

I admire you: your heart, your faith, and the person you are. I'm grateful for every conversation, every laugh, and every moment you've chosen to share with me. And I truly hope we can keep making meaningful memories.

So I'll say it simply, the way I've wanted to for a long time: I like you.

But please know this, too: I'm not writing this to ask anything of you. You don't owe me an answer or any feelings in return. Whatever our connection means to you is yours to decide, and I will respect it completely. I simply wanted you to know that you've become a special part of my life, and that alone is something I'm thankful for.

With all sincerity,

A memory from Mawab

The Girl From My Dreams

Long before I met you, there was someone I used to imagine in my dreams. I never really knew what she looked like. She was simply a faceless figure, someone whose presence I could only picture in my imagination.

Then, years later, I met you.

And sometimes, whenever I see your photographs, especially the ones where you're facing away or your face isn't completely shown, I'm reminded of that old dream.

It's difficult to explain, but there's something beautiful about how someone I once imagined can remind me so much of someone real.

From one friend in the faith to another

The last page

A Quiet Hope

Maybe someday, I'll get to see you again, not just in a picture on my screen, but right in front of me.

Maybe we'll have more opportunities to talk, laugh, share stories, and create memories that we can look back on someday.

Until then, thank you for the conversations, the laughter, the encouragement, and the little moments that became meaningful to me.

You might not realize how much those simple interactions have meant, but I genuinely appreciate them.

You were someone I noticed for just a moment, but somehow, you've become someone whose presence has remained in my thoughts and memories for years.

I don't know what the future holds, but I hope it gives us more opportunities to know each other better.

Until then, I'll keep this memory close to my heart.

With sincerity,

Someone who still remembers that day.
